Review scores and category rank are signals for selecting references; they do not prove a flow is best. Adoption counts and detected installations are not automatically market share. Compare products by the specific task, identify denominators and source dates, and distinguish direct visual inspection from documented behavior. Preserve uncertainty where public evidence is incomplete.

Campaign's current logo, icons and login remain the visual baseline. Competitors guide task structure and behavior; they do not replace ten98 branding. Shared UI is built here with tokens and typed components. Product engines, data, permissions, reconciliation and releases belong to each app session and the human owner. Follow [the bounded swarm gates](../SWARM.md).

## Implementation status

All six report sets are complete, with 18 evidence/flow/recommendation documents. Their source limitations and unaccepted feature suggestions remain recorded in each report. Three shared flow compositions are implemented as local catalogue references using current exports: connection verification, data investigation and change review/reconciliation. They do not approve new product capabilities. Specialized editors, canvases and domain features remain proposed until their use case and data contract are agreed.

## Accepted shared work in this wave

The coordinator selected three cross-suite compositions already covered by existing UX rules: connection verification, data investigation and scoped change review/reconciliation. They use existing library exports, include explicit demo outcomes and complete TSX, and are local catalogue recipes. App teams can review them before requesting a stable shared API. No workflow runtime, rich-text engine, telephony client, hypervisor or domain-specific backend was added.

All larger specialized editors and new feature suggestions remain in the reports' decision registers. The next coding worker needs a concrete approved task and consumer contract; it does not automatically start another parity or research wave.
